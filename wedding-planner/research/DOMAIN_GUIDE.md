# Indian wedding domain guide

Research baseline: 15 October 2026. Authority: [project intent](../README.md). Source IDs and limitations: [research index](README.md). This guide covers the celebration before arrival, during the three days, and through departure and settlement. It supplies questions and options; it does not select this family's arrangements or prescribe application modules.

## 1. The guest's complete journey

First-party planners connect invitations, RSVP, stays, transfers, hospitality and special needs [S02, S03]. The proposed coverage below translates that observation into questions a family should be able to answer.

| Stage | Guest needs to know | Family needs to understand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation | Whose wedding; exact dates; which functions; city; warm invitation | Correct spellings, approved wording, audience and source of truth |
| Deciding/travel | Arrival expectations, venue addresses, whom to contact | Who is coming, who is tentative, travel/stay support and deadlines |
| Reaching the city | Station/airport/terminal, pickup meeting point, contact | Arrival groups, actual ETA, vehicle/luggage needs and receiver |
| Check-in | Where to go, room readiness, meal/rest arrangements | Room allocation, check-in conditions, early arrivals, assistance |
| Attending functions | Date/time, arrival time, precise venue entrance, practical attire/custom notes if needed | Audience, travel, preparation, meal windows and venue readiness |
| Between functions | Water, tea/snack/meal availability, rest, changing and help | Guests remaining at hotel, children/elders, long gaps and staff meals |
| Returning at night | Return transport and a reachable host | Last shuttle, guests still at venue, late arrivals and luggage |
| Vidai/departure | Next-day food, checkout, bags and onward travel | Departures, room release, packed meals if requested and belongings |
| Afterward | Thanks and approved photo sharing | Vendor settlement, deliveries, returns and unresolved issues |

Public invitation information may include confirmed event facts, map pins, an approved contact, practical guidance and a countdown to an explicitly named occasion. Private guest records, travel manifests, room numbers, dietary/health details, payments and internal decisions stay with authorized family. A public invitation does not imply every internal function is public.

## 3. Functions: an option catalogue, not a fixed Indian template

Planners commonly describe engagement, mehendi, haldi, sangeet, baraat and a main ceremony, with different regional orders [S01]. This project's baraat-to-vidai scope does not establish every other event. Distinguish **arrival of the visiting wedding party** from the **ceremonial baraat procession**; they may be separate moments.

| Possible occasion | Planning questions | Applicability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wedding party arrival/welcome | Arrival group, receiving family, luggage, welcome food, rooms | Confirm whether this is the project's opening event |
| Engagement/roka/sagai | Already completed? Separate ceremony or combined? | Optional; may fall outside these three days |
| Family prayers/local customs | Name, participants, officiant/elder, articles and preparation | Only the family's actual customs |
| Haldi | Participants, washable setting, clothes/change, towels and cleanup | Optional; timing and form vary |
| Mehendi | Bride/family/guest artists, booking duration, hand-use limitations, meal assistance | Optional; may be before arrival |
| Sangeet/music/family evening | Performers, rehearsal, running order, sound and finish | Optional; can remain a small family gathering |
| Wedding-day preparation | Dressing/makeup, photography, meals, attendants and travel | Times from actual services and family needs |
| Baraat procession/welcome/milni | Starting point, route, duration, receiver, venue agreement | Custom and venue dependent |
| Varmala/jaimala | Placement in programme, participants, stage access and coverage | Confirm exact family practice |
| Main marriage ceremony | Officiant-approved name, rites, order, duration and timing window | Never infer required rites from a generic checklist |
| Wedding meal/reception/blessings | Audience, service window, seating and portrait time | May be combined, separate, before or after rites |
| Vidai | Expected timing, essential family, vehicle, belongings and onward plan | Established project scope; details unconfirmed |
| Departure brunch/checkout | Guests staying afterward, breakfast, luggage and transfers | When relevant to actual stays |
| Griha pravesh/other later customs | Owning family, location, participants | Only if family includes it within planning scope |

Indian weddings include many community and religious traditions. A nikah, Anand Karaj, church ceremony, civil ceremony or regional rite must be represented on its own confirmed terms if relevant. This pack has not researched each tradition's religious or legal requirements. It supplies no universal auspicious time, ritual shopping list or registration conclusion.

## 4. Food: assess the whole stay, not just the grand dinner

Catering sources illustrate regional/familiar food and parallel dietary provision [S04], and possible live stations [S05]. The menu options and balance principles below are proposed planning aids. **Examples are vegetarian for a useful initial catalogue; the actual wedding food policy is unknown.** They are alternatives, not a prescription to serve every category.

### 4.1 Complete meal coverage

Consider: arrival refreshments; each occupied day's breakfast; lunch; afternoon tea/snack; function starters/stalls; dinner; late-night food where needed; departure breakfast/packed food if requested; water throughout; vendor/driver/helper meals. A hotel breakfast package and venue breakfast are not automatically two separate meals for the same guests.

For each service establish date/window, place, people actually present, guest/staff counts, menu, service format, dietary arrangements, who provides it, what the price includes, and the confirmation state. A function RSVP, a meal count, a vehicle manifest and an overnight count are different views of people.

### 4.2 Variety catalogue

| Role | Illustrative alternatives | Check across the three days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakfast anchor | Idli-sambar; poha; upma; dhokla; paratha-curd | Rotate the anchor; support guests who prefer a simpler breakfast |
| Tea-time snack | Khandvi; mini kachori; vegetable sandwich; corn chaat | Avoid making every snack a heavy fried meal |
| Welcome drinks | Nimbu pani; jaljeera; kokum drink; chaas | Water remains easy to get; verify ice/water source |
| Chaat station | Pani puri; sev puri; bhel; tikki chaat | Several versions can repeat the same potato/curd/chutney profile |
| Grill starter | Paneer tikka; vegetable skewers; corn; mushroom tikka | Provide variety beyond paneer; cooking/holding and allergens matter |
| Familiar vegetable | Seasonal dry sabzi; bhindi; cabbage-peas; mixed vegetables | Include a recognizable lower-spice choice where requested |
| Rich main | Paneer preparation; malai kofta; regional festive sabzi | Different names may still mean similar rich gravies |
| Pulse/protein main | Dal tadka; chana; rajma; regional dal | Balance alongside dairy-rich items; assess guest preference |
| Grain/rice | Steamed rice; jeera rice; vegetable pulao; khichdi | Not every meal needs a festive rice dish |
| Breads | Phulka; tandoori roti; puri; naan | Warm service, queue capacity and paired dishes |
| Regional meal identity | Gujarati thali elements; Maharashtrian favourites; Rajasthani set; South Indian spread | Choose based on the families and caterer's capability |
| Live substantial food | Dosa; pav bhaji; pasta; rolls | These may already serve as a main; avoid duplicate buffet versions |
| Lighter accompaniment | Salad; fruit; simple raita; soup | Recipe and handling still require verification |
| Dessert | Jalebi; gulab jamun; shrikhand; halwa; kulfi; fruit | Contrast temperature/texture and richness instead of only increasing count |
| Child/elder-friendly option | Mild dal-rice; soft khichdi; idli; plain bread | Preference-based hospitality; no medical suitability is implied |
| Dietary parallel | Guest-approved Jain, no onion/garlic, vegan, fasting or allergy-aware preparation | These are distinct requirements; verify recipe and preparation separately |

### 4.3 How to judge balance

Look at exact dish repetition **and** repeated ingredients, gravy bases, preparation, richness, textures and cuisines. A repeat can be intentional: tea, plain rice, a simple dal or a favourite requested twice may improve comfort. The goal is a satisfying stay, not zero repeated ingredients.

Judge stalls together with the full meal. Paneer tikka, paneer roll and two paneer gravies can produce low experiential variety despite four dish names. A live dosa station plus a large buffet needs an explicit service decision; adding stations can add queues, equipment and staffing rather than useful variety.

Use a meaningful family taste comparison: which of two complete three-day proposals would people actually enjoy, and why? Record accepted reasons and exceptions; an abstract score does not establish taste.

### 4.4 Illustrative pacing, not this wedding's schedule

| Stay period | Possible meal character | Complementary idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival/Day 1 breakfast | Familiar, convenient welcome | One breakfast anchor plus tea/fruit |
| Day 1 lunch | Regional/family identity | Balanced home-style meal |
| Day 1 evening/dinner | Social/interactive | Selected stalls with a complementary dinner |
| Day 2 breakfast | Different anchor | Avoid copying the previous morning wholesale |
| Day 2 lunch | Comfortable between functions | Lighter option and a complete dietary alternative |
| Day 2 evening/dinner | A distinct festive meal | Change cuisine/preparation emphasis |
| Day 3 breakfast | Reliable before preparations | Known service time and simple alternative |
| Day 3 main meal | Family-approved celebration spread | Signature dishes without duplicating every earlier station |
| After vidai/departure | Food for people still present | Breakfast/late meal/packed food as actually needed |

Do not infer exact dishes, quantities, a compulsory number of courses or a food budget from this illustration. Caterer tasting and family acceptance settle choices.

### 4.5 Dietary trust and service readiness

Confirm the guest's actual restrictions; Jain, no onion/garlic, fasting, vegetarian, vegan and allergen avoidance are not interchangeable labels. Recipe names alone do not establish ingredients. A dairy-free label cannot be inferred for a paneer-based dish; a wheat-free recipe does not establish cross-contact control.

FSSAI guidance covers potable water, hygiene and food handling [S06]. Its historical catering provisions explicitly address allergen identification, cross-contact and disclosure [S07]. Proposed planning checks: ask the caterer to verify ingredients and preparation/serving controls, explain uncertainty honestly, and agree on an appropriate alternative with the guest. A personal allergy detail remains private. The app must not certify food safety, prescribe diets, or treat live cooking as proof of safety.

Also resolve buffet opening/closing, replenishment, elder assistance, stations' practical throughput, kitchen equipment/power, covered service, drinking water and waste handling. Any numerical service assumption needs the caterer's own evidence.

## 5. Guests, stays and travel

### 5.1 People and attendance

Use a real person as the counting unit while preserving useful household/group context. A shared phone number is not proof of duplicate identity. Keep separate event attendance, dietary requests, stay nights and transfer needs. Unknown is different from zero or declined; tentative people stay visibly tentative. Track actual changes without silently overwriting a confirmed decision.

### 5.2 Accommodation questions

Hotel check-in/out and accessible facilities are property-specific [S09]. These proposed checks prevent a room list from giving false reassurance:

| Concern | What to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Night-by-night demand | Every occupied night, peak simultaneous rooms, late-night arrivals and departure day |
| Room suitability | Adults/children, confirmed occupancy rules, bed type, extra beds/cots, family sharing preferences |
| Early arrival | Actual arrival time versus guaranteed room readiness; prior-night room or agreed waiting/rest/change arrangement |
| Late departure | Checkout versus train/flight; luggage storage, changing/rest space and charges |
| Access and comfort | Step-free route through entrance, room and washroom; lift availability; distance/noise/rest needs |
| Confirmation | Requested, reserved, assigned and ready are different; identify vendor confirmation |
| Cost and conditions | Who pays, meals included, extra occupancy, taxes, release/cancellation terms and incidental charges |
| Coordination | One reachable hospitality owner, hotel contact and private rooming information |

**Derived arithmetic, not a market benchmark:** eight rooms for two nights plus four additional rooms for one of those nights equals **20 room-nights**, with **12 rooms at peak**. Twenty room-nights are not twenty rooms available at once. Number of wedding days alone cannot determine required nights.

### 5.3 Transport questions

Resolve arrival/departure points and terminals; live ETA versus scheduled time; group and luggage; vehicle's confirmed usable passenger seats; driver/contact; receiver; pickup sign/meeting point; hotel/function transfers; return journey; loading, traffic and turnaround; parking and accessible boarding where needed. Do not assume a nearby hotel supplies an airport shuttle. Research actual journey times only after locations and timing are known.

A vehicle can serve two transfers sequentially only when the full first journey, unloading and return/repositioning leave enough time. Fifteen usable seats cannot carry nineteen passengers in one trip. A new arrival may change rooms, meals and pickups together.

## 6. Functions, venues and people working behind them

Venue capacities vary with configuration [S08]. Use the actual proposed seating/stage/mandap/catering layout, event audience and venue confirmation. Never convert a standing reception maximum into seated meal capacity.

For each applicable function consider: approved timing, fixed ceremony window if any, guest arrival, audience, space, owner/contact, preparation and travel, vendor arrival/setup, food service, closing and room turnaround. A change should reveal affected commitments; automatic rescheduling of a religious timing is inappropriate.

| Arrangement | Proposed practical questions |
| --- | --- |
| Ritual readiness | Which officiant/elder approved the sequence and articles? Who brings them? What is still missing? |
| Couple/family preparation | Are dressing/makeup, photographer and travel times compatible? Who carries essential clothes/jewellery/articles? |
| Decor | What is built where; when can access start; what is reused; where is it stored; who dismantles/returns it? |
| Entertainment | Is the running order practical; are performers present; has sound/power been agreed; who handles cues? |
| Photography | Who covers simultaneous events; which family groups matter; are sharing preferences clear; what is delivered when? |
| Hospitality | Who is welcoming, receiving arrivals, helping elders, checking meals, and closing the venue? Is one person double-booked? |
| Gifts/belongings | Which households/people receive what; how many packs; who holds items; what must return? |

Photography sources distinguish ceremony, portrait and film deliverables [S12]. Exact coverage/delivery belongs in the selected vendor agreement; no standard turnaround is promised here. Do not assume drones, fireworks, alcohol, processions or amplified entertainment are part of this wedding. If chosen, resolve venue and applicable local conditions before commitment.

## 7. Money, decisions and recovery

The following are proposed coordination checks, not prices, contractual advice or compulsory workflows:

- Compare quotes against the same scope: functions, people, hours, staff, equipment, travel, food/stays, taxes, overtime, exclusions and deliverables. A lower headline amount can cover less.
- Distinguish estimate, agreed total, approved extras, paid amount, refundable deposit and balance. Paying a deposit does not increase the contracted total. Do not invent a tax rate or contingency percentage.
- Record a clear owner and relevant deadline for an unresolved dependency. A family member should understand the next decision without reading a project-management manual.
- A proposed venue/menu/time change remains a proposal until approved. Show its consequences for guest communication, catering, rooms, transport and cost; confirm supplier acknowledgment where needed. A publish action is distinct from a draft edit.
- For an outdoor plan, consider an actual covered alternative and the capacity/setup needed to use it. Use location/date-appropriate IMD information when available [S11], not an unrelated current forecast. Resolve power, transport or supplier failures with proportionate fallback arrangements.
- Access needs apply to the whole guest route, not just a venue entrance. Official physical-accessibility guidance is a reference point [S10]; a site visit and venue confirmation establish actual suitability.
- Registration/documentation, if included, follows the applicable local process. Government portals provide jurisdiction-specific services [S14]; this research has not established this couple's legal requirements.

After vidai, account for remaining guests, checkout, transfers, food, lost belongings, rental returns, receipts/deposits, vendor balances and media delivery. Completion of the ceremony is not proof that hospitality has finished.

## 8. What the experience should feel like

A guest can understand whose celebration it is, where to go and when, and how to get help. A family member can identify what is settled, what needs a decision and whom to contact. Both should retain the warmth of a family celebration. The concrete experience matters more than dashboard density or feature count.

Web checks should include readable event information independent of decorative images, keyboard operation, reflow/zoom, labelled controls and appropriate contrast [S15]. Private data needs actual access enforcement, including against an authenticated person who is not authorized family [S16]. These are evaluation outcomes; Codex chooses an implementation consistent with repository guidance.

Keep visual taste review separate from functional correctness. Use family-approved comparisons and real rendered evidence; technical QA cannot establish that a design feels personal or beautiful [S20]. No visual direction has been chosen or rated by this research.
